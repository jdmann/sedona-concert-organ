The First Instrument

The Claremont
Estey

In 1931 the Estey Organ Company of Brattleboro, Vermont installed a new and very large pipe organ in Bridges Auditorium at Pomona College. The Organ Historical Society records it simply as Estey’s largest pipe organ. It has been donated to the Sedona Conservatory, and it will form half of the new instrument.

A memorial in sound

Built for a daughter
who never heard it

In 1930 the Claremont Colleges commissioned a large concert organ from the Estey Organ Company of Brattleboro, Vermont for the school’s new Auditorium of Music. The firm’s tonal director and noted organ architect James B. Jamison had recently returned from a tour to Europe where he heard, studied, and documented each country’s school of organbuilding. Jamison writes:

“As I went from country to country and heard, tried and studied the best features of national schools of organ design, it seemed to me that no one of them was entirely right or comprehensive, but that a judicious blend of the best features of American, English, French and German practices would result in the most superior of organs, with the broadest tonal palette of all, and without question the most enjoyable of instruments, as well as the most majestic.”

Jamison’s research tour included Henry Willis & Sons (St. Paul’s Cathedral, London), Harrison & Harrison (Westminster Abbey, London), Noel Bonovia-Hunt (esteemed organ designer and voicer), the Cavaillé-Coll factory (Notre Dame Cathedral and St. Sulpice, Paris), and the organs of Gottfried Silbermann (Hofkirche, Dresden and Freiberg Cathedral).

The Claremont instrument was the first American concert hall organ to comprehensively employ this international design. Other organ builders began to adopt this concept which eventually became known as ‘The American Classic.’ This tonal design concept has served as the basis of innumerable instruments since then, as well as for several celebrated organs including Riverside Church in New York, Boston Symphony Hall, and the Salt Lake City Tabernacle.

Not only were the designs of the various European organbuilding schools employed in the Claremont organ, but several sets of pipes were actually designed and built by the European builders who were key in developing those concepts. The Trompette was built by the renowned Aristide Cavaillé-Coll of France. The heroic Tuba Stenora was built by Harrison & Harrison of Durham, England and is a duplicate of the same voice in the Durham Cathedral. Two main sets of “Mixture” pipes are copies of pipes built by Gottfried Silbermann, a personal friend of J. S. Bach.

Added to these European styles is the organ’s ‘Solo’ division featuring American ‘Symphonic’ voices such as Violin, Viola, French Horn, Orchestral Trumpet and Oboe, and a unique Saxophone.

Weston Harris, Organ Crafter, for the Sedona Conservatory — source

Chronicle

From Brattleboro
to the red rocks

  1. 1907

    Mabel Shaw Bridges dies

    The memorial that would one day become an auditorium and an organ begins with a private grief.

  2. 1930

    Commissioned by the Claremont Colleges

    A large concert organ is commissioned from the Estey Organ Company of Brattleboro, Vermont for the school’s new Auditorium of Music — the gift of Mr. and Mrs. Appleton Bridges in memory of their daughter.

  3. 1931

    The specification is drawn

    Joseph W. Clokey, the college organist, and James B. Jamison of the Estey Organ Company write the stoplist together, incorporating the results of Jamison’s recent study of instruments by the leading builders of Germany, England and France.

  4. 23 Nov 1931

    The dedicatory recital

    Palmer Christian of the University of Michigan plays the opening recital at the console.

  5. The century after

    A hall’s voice

    The instrument serves Bridges Auditorium and the Claremont Colleges — convocations, commencements, recitals — for the better part of a century.

  6. Recently

    Donated to Sedona

    Through the mediation of the organ’s curator, Harris, the college donates the monumental instrument to the Sedona Conservatory.

  7. Now

    Preparing for Arizona

    The Claremont instrument is currently being prepared for its transport to Arizona. Its magnificent 4-keyboard console will be completely refurbished and playable in its own right.
