The First Instrument

The Claremont
Estey

In 1931 the Estey Organ Company of Brattleboro, Vermont installed a new and very large pipe organ in Bridges Auditorium at Pomona College. The Organ Historical Society records it simply as Estey’s largest pipe organ. It has been donated to the Sedona Conservatory, and it will form half of the new instrument.

A memorial in sound

Built for a daughter
who never heard it

In 1931 the Estey Organ Company installed a new, large pipe organ in the Bridges Auditorium of Pomona College, Claremont, CA. The auditorium and the organ were the gifts of Mr. and Mrs. Appleton Bridges in memory of their daughter Mabel Shaw Bridges who had died in 1907.

Joseph W. Clokey, college organist, and James B. Jamison of the Estey company drew up the specifications for the organ. The organ incorporated the results of Mr. Jamison’s recent study of instruments built by prominent organ builders in Germany, England and France.

The Claremont organ is a notable example of an international ensemble in that the best American tonal work has been supplemented by the salient tonal specialties of the English and French schools of organ design.

The dedicatory recital of the Estey Organ built for Claremont Colleges, Claremont, Cal., took place Nov. 23, with Palmer Christian of the University of Michigan at the console.

Through the mediation of Harris, who is the organ’s curator, the college has graciously donated this monumental instrument to the Sedona Conservatory.

Organ Historical Society — source

Chronicle

From Brattleboro
to the red rocks

  1. 1907

    Mabel Shaw Bridges dies

    The memorial that would one day become an auditorium and an organ begins with a private grief.

  2. 1931

    A gift to Claremont

    Mr. and Mrs. Appleton Bridges give Bridges Auditorium — and the organ within it — to Pomona College in memory of their daughter.

  3. 1931

    The specification is drawn

    Joseph W. Clokey, the college organist, and James B. Jamison of the Estey Organ Company write the stoplist together, incorporating the results of Jamison’s recent study of instruments by the leading builders of Germany, England and France.

  4. 23 Nov 1931

    The dedicatory recital

    Palmer Christian of the University of Michigan plays the opening recital at the console.

  5. The century after

    A hall’s voice

    The instrument serves Bridges Auditorium and the Claremont Colleges — convocations, commencements, recitals — for the better part of a century.

  6. Recently

    Donated to Sedona

    Through the mediation of the organ’s curator, Harris, the college donates the monumental instrument to the Sedona Conservatory.

  7. Now

    Awaiting its second life

    In Sedona, sharing quarters with the Oberlin Skinner. Its grand ornate four-keyboard console is to be refurbished and playable in its own right.
