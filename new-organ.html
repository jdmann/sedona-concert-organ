The Instrument to Come

The Sedona
Concert Organ

Not a new organ so much as a marriage of two old ones. The Claremont Estey and the Oberlin Skinner are being restored side by side and placed under a single new console — and the sum will be, by a wide margin, the largest concert hall organ in the world.

The union

One player,
two instruments

These historic instruments will be handled in unique ways befitting their joint pedigree. Instead of being moved to a distant business warehouse and factory for conventional refurbishing as is typically done, these organs will, rather, be approached from a curatorship perspective. This includes their re-assembly in an ‘Organ Pavilion’ building in Sedona. Here, each of the organs will be revived so that its tonal composition and mechanics might be experienced and evaluated.

The Sedona Organ Pavilion will serve as a center for their rebirth and refurbishing, as well as provide public opportunities to experience the completed instrument prior to its installation.

The Estey organ’s magnificent 4-keyboard console will be completely refurbished, as will be E. M. Skinner’s original 1915 Oberlin console reconfigured in 1955 by Aeolian-Skinner. From each console one may play its respective fully re-established instrument. In addition, a new 5-keyboard Sedona Conservatory Concert Organ console will be created to play the entire Conservatory Organ as a single, fully integrated instrument.

Chronicle

What happens
next

The organbuilding project is under way. This is the order of the work.

  1. Complete

    Acquisition

    Both landmark organs located, secured, and brought to Sedona — the Estey from Claremont and the Aeolian-Skinner from Oberlin.

  2. Under way

    The new console

    Conception, design and construction of the grand Concert Hall console: five premium keyboards, 300 stops, and the control systems that let one player command both historic organs as a single instrument.

  3. Next

    The two historic consoles

    Refurbishing of the grand ornate four-keyboard Claremont console and of the historic Oberlin console, so that each organ may also be played on its own.

  4. Then

    The organ case

    The visible pipes and structural elements will extend across the full width of the concert hall stage — intended as a singular sculptural masterpiece in its own right.

  5. 3–5 years

    The building of it

    Restoration, re-leathering, revoicing, winding, and installation of some 235 ranks across the divisions of the finished instrument.

  6. Then

    First sound in the hall

    More than 12,700 pipes speak together for the first time, in its new home.
