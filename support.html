Beyond a pipe

Buy a full
rank of pipes

Each level below is followed by a list of unique tonal colors. These names each represent a full set of pipes, from the tallest low-pitched giants to the shortest high-pitched pipes. While the organ contains many more pipe sets than those offered here, these are some of the most unique and deserving of individual full-set sponsorship. Donor plaques will permanently document every donation listed.

$1,000 7 pipe sets

Nachthorn

German “Horn of the Night” with a calm, mellow tone.

Block Flute

A classic, full-sounding German flute.

Tierce

Rich in harmonic overtones, this provides a bright tonal color often used in French repertoire.

Sponsored 21 March 2018

Sifflöte

The highest pitched stop (set of pipes) in the organ providing a sparkling tone. The highest pitched pipes are only 1/2″ long.

Cello

Played with the foot pedals for a deep, melodic string tone.

Gedeckt Pommer

A crisp bass tone similar to a clear, rich-toned string bass.

Wood Flute

A sparkling wood flute.

$5,000 12 pipe sets

Piccolo

Like the instrument in the orchestra.

Viola

A smooth, broad tone similar to the orchestral instrument.

Horn

Of unusual European design with the tone of the early Basset Horn.

Sesquialtera II

Features two sets of baroque-style flute pipes. This is used in period baroque and French literature.

Quint

A specialty voice that binds the higher tones of the organ together.

Quinte

This binds the deeper bass tones of the organ together.

Rohr Flute

An elegant German wood flute. “Rohr” means that it has a bit of a nasal quality.

Forest Flute

A bright, cheerful “sprite” flute.

Violin Diapason

A full, poignant string tone.

Silver Flute

A clear “silver-toned” flute noted in reviews as unusually exquisite.

Gemshorn

A pastoral, “gem of a horn” tone. The pipes have a tapered “chimney” shape.

Choral Bass

A staple in any professional performance instrument. This stop is in the Pedal Division, played with the foot pedals.

$10,000 10 pipe sets

Quintaten

This is a specialized neo-baroque tone. This one is a pristine example of this unusual voice. The pipes have a special appearance with a wide red felt strip around its top.

Holz Gedackt

This Germanic flute is in the “Great Organ” division — the main section of the organ. It has a slight “chiff” at the beginning of the tone that gives added clarity to intricate contrapuntal music, e.g. Bach fugues.

Nazat Flute

These pipes were originally in the Aeolian-Skinner organ at St. Bartholomew’s Church in New York City. It is a tart baroque flute for use in early period music such as Handel concertos, Bach fugues.

Orchestral Oboe

Like the orchestral instrument.

Bassoon

Like the orchestral instrument.

Hautbois

A Germanic-style Oboe with a pointed bright tone. The vibrating brass reed inside these pipes functions like the reed in a Clarinet.

Spitz Flute

The shape of these pipes is pointed (German “Spitz”). This gives them a light flute tone. Ernest Skinner, builder of the 1915 Oberlin organ, included this voice in most of his noted instruments.

Vox Humana

The “human voice”. A very distinctive tone, similar to a choir singing from a distance. This set, from the Claremont organ, features the trademark carved wood reed base giving it a distinctly earthy tone.

Musette

These pipes have a tone similar to bagpipes. It was added to the Claremont organ after its initial installation.

Gong

Concert level organs often feature a number of percussion voices. This is a Chinese gong.

$15,000 16 pipe sets

Solo Gamba II

A Gamba is a strong string tone. This stop features two sets of pipes having a rich and bold solo Viola tone.

Orchestral Strings II

These are from the 1915 Oberlin organ by Ernest Skinner. He created and specialized in these ethereal string tones.

Clarinet

This very rich Clarinet tone is a perfect example of the prized orchestral organ voices built by Ernest Skinner.

Clarinette

Lighter than the Skinner set above. These two types of Clarinet tone will be “dialogued” across the expanse of the organ space.

Harp

A percussion voice with metal bars struck by piano-style hammers. Similar to the marimba but with a more plucked tone — like a harp.

Rohr Schalmei

A classic baroque-style stop used in a wide range of music. It is a bright version of a shepherd’s pipe, and uses very low air pressure.

Krummhorn

A classic organ voice in the baroque “Positiv” section of the organ. It has a pointed clarinet-style tone.

Concert Flute

This style of the classic organ stop was invented by Ernest Skinner, the original builder of the Oberlin organ.

Cor Anglais

English Horn — a premium version of this enchanting orchestral instrument.

Solo Orchestral Trumpet

A perfect example of a brilliant Solo Trumpet.

Solo Major Flute

Noted in reviews as a perfect Solo Flute. Built with thick wood and played on high air pressure, giving unusual presence, strength and stability.

Rankett

A bass tone in baroque style — bright and crisp so that it remains clear in intricate contrapuntal music.

Solo First Violins II

Two sets of very bright violin-toned pipes used in American and all romantic-style music. Very thin and long, made of shiny tin.

Full Mixture

Provides colour harmonics to the pedal bass pipes. Made of pure tin.

Scharff

German for “sharp”. A mix of 3 pipe sets (183 individual pipes) balanced to provide a telling, brilliant tone.

Cymbal

Two sets of high-pitched pipes which mix together to sound like a tinkling cymbal.

$25,000 23 pipe sets

Saxophone

An instrumental voice specially created for the Claremont organ, sounding like a solo Saxophone.

Tromba

Trombone-style pipes built in England by the noted Harrison and Harrison of Durham. Their tone is pure English cathedral.

Cavaillé-Coll Strings II

The great French master built organs in Notre Dame, St. Sulpice and others. These strings were built from models provided by Cavaillé-Coll.

Harrison Solo Stentorphone

Designed and built in England, and reviewed as a most perfect stentorian voice.

Noel Bonavia-Hunt Diapason

Signed. Bonavia-Hunt was recognised as the preeminent specialist in English organ tone. This set was designed, built and signed by him.

Plein Jeu IV

Imported from Europe for the Oberlin organ, made of pure tin. Four sets (244 pipes) mixing to provide bright harmonics in the Swell division.

16′ Wood Open Diapason

Monumental wood pipes at 16′ tall and 20″ square, providing the grand bass tone under the full organ.

16′ Dulciana

A pristine set of metal 16′ pipes providing a quiet bass “purr” tone.

16′ Diaphone

Large wood bass pipes designed in England by the creator of this style. A powerful tuba-like bass tone on very high air pressure.

Kleine Erzahler II

This sweet, plaintive tone was created by Ernest Skinner. Perfect examples built by him for the Oberlin organ.

Deagan Orchestral Chimes

Orchestral chimes struck by hammers activated from the console. Deagan was the premium builder of chimes at the time.

Mounted Cornet V

A grand solo voice of French origin with five sets of pipes, “mounted” up front so the sound is direct and clear.

32′ Bombarde

Thunderous 32′ bass pipes sounding with a Trombone tone under the entire organ — two octaves below middle C.

Great Schulze Diapason

Built in England, this is the core tone of the entire organ, of English “Schulze” design created by Noel Bonavia-Hunt.

Great Organ Trumpets III

Three sets of trumpets at the core of the “powerhouse” Great division. Noted in reviews as stellar.

Skinner Voix Humaine

A specialty of Ernest Skinner, and this set is of extraordinary vintage quality and beauty.

32′ Soubasse

Wood pipes providing a bass “purr” that you feel vibrating more than a tone you hear.

32′ Mahogany Bassoon

Deep bass pipes made of exquisite red mahogany, with a quiet yet distinct bassoon tone.

Main Air Blower

The organ’s voice is made possible by air pressure. A large “Orgoblo” unit from the Claremont organ with a 7½ hp motor.

Operating Systems

The relays, air valves, air hoses, wiring and woodwork.

Kleine Mixtur

A “small mixture” of 4 sets of pipes which mix together for classic intimate harmonics.

Cavaillé-Coll Choir Flutes III

Classic French colour flutes built from models provided by the famed French organ builder.

Montre

These 16′ metal pipes were part of the gold pipes seen along the front of the Oberlin organ. They are in French style.

$50,000 17 pipe sets

Tuba Sonora

Caps the entire organ as the imperial grand solo voice. Built by Harrison and Harrison of Durham, a duplicate of their Tuba in Durham Cathedral, celebrated as the finest in England.

Trumpet En Chamade

Triumphant trumpet pipes placed in full view and horizontal, like heralding trumpeters, sounding out brilliantly over the entire hall.

Cavaillé-Coll Trompette

Built directly by the great Cavaillé-Coll and given to William Haskell, tonal director at Estey. Reviewed as the perfect French-style trumpet.

32′ Trombone

The largest trumpet-style bass pipes in the organ, 32′ long. Their heroic tone completes the full bass of the organ.

32′ Major Bass

The first ever built of its kind. William Haskell conceived and built these gigantic wood pipes for the Claremont organ.

Main Tower in Case

The visible pipes will form a spectacular sculpture suggesting the viewscape of the Sedona region. The centre tower will be the pinnacle of the layout.

Grand Harmonics VI

Magnificent pipes (366 individual pipes) which cap the full tone of the organ.

Stentor Harmonics IV

Four sets of pipes used to give a chorus of Trumpets a defined edge and snap.

32′ Violone

A set of 32′ gold pipes displayed across the front of the Oberlin organ. It will be a primary feature in the Conservatory’s organ case.

Prinzipal Chorus

A group of primary or “principal” voices which together form a cohesive sound-scape “chorus” in the Germanic style.

Harrison Diapason Chorus

This chorus of primary voices, designed in England, is in the English “cathedral” style.

Schulze Principal Chorus

Built in Europe, from the “Schulze” school of organ tone recognised as the finest chorus style in the English tradition.

Silbermann Mixture

Six pipe sets modelled on those built by Gottfried Silbermann (1683–1753). This voice has been called a “sheet of silvery flame”.

Neo-Baroque Great Organ

Clear, transparent voices useful in the full range of organ literature that requires distinct clarity.

Neo-Baroque Positiv Organ

The most petite and crisp voices in the organ, used in all varieties of music, particularly the authentic baroque.

Neo-Baroque Pedal Organ

Provides light and very articulate bass tone.

Record/Playback System

An automatic playback feature so the organ can be experienced and demonstrated when an organist is not available, and to record performances.

$100,000 10 pipe sets

Claremont Console

Refurbishing of the grand ornate 4-keyboard console of the Claremont Organ.

Oberlin Console

Refurbishing of the historic organ console from Oberlin College/Conservatory.

Great Division

The primary division, featuring a variety of the most full-bodied tones in the Conservatory organ.

Swell Division

The largest number of tonal colors, from the most subtle and sweet flutes to robust string tones, then brilliant European horns and authentic French trumpets.

Choir Division

A colour section with French “cornets” and flutes, Clarinets, English Horn, Oboe, Musette, Bassoon, Harp.

Positiv Division

A complete set of voices in the articulate, German baroque style of the 16th and 17th centuries.

Solo Division

Americana, with French Horns, Solo Violins, orchestral chimes, soaring solo Flutes and Trumpets.

Sedona Trumpet Division

The horizontal trumpets extending into the hall — like the heralding trumpeters seen on festive occasions.

Pedal Division

All the bass tones in the organ, from many 32′ and 16′ tall pipes.

Neo-Baroque Division

The crisp, articulate, and clear/transparent tone of the organ.

$125,000 1 pipe set

Console keyboards, stops and controls

The console will feature 5 premium keyboards, 300 stops, and associated control systems.

$150,000 2 pipe sets

Full Sedona Conservatory Console

For conception, design, and construction of the grand Concert Hall console.

Sedona Conservatory Organ Case

The visible pipes and structural elements of the organ extending across the full width of the stage will be a singular sculptural masterpiece. The case design will be inspired by and will visually suggest viewscapes from the Sedona region.
