The Second Instrument

The Oberlin
Skinner

Installed in Finney Chapel in 1955, Aeolian-Skinner Opus 230-A taught organists at the Oberlin College Conservatory of Music for four decades. It left Ohio in 1999, waited fifteen years in an Indianapolis workshop, and came to Sedona in 2015.

The American Classic

Clarity over weight

The Finney Aeolian-Skinner organ incorporated pipe sets from the historic 1915 E. M. Skinner instrument that preceded it, and had served multiple generations of Conservatory organ students and concert artists.

In 1997 Oberlin College received a significant bequest to purchase a large new instrument of a tonal style not yet part of Oberlin’s organscape. With the choice being a French-styled instrument, and with the only other concert hall large enough to house the gifted instrument having just received a significant new organ by the Dutch builder Flentrop, Finney Chapel was selected to receive the new installation.

The instrument was sold to a congregation in the Episcopal Diocese of Virginia, and in 1999 was moved from Oberlin’s Finney Chapel to the Indianapolis organ builders Goulding and Wood.

Oberlin’s Aeolian-Skinner Opus #230-A was purchased for the Sedona Conservatory in June, 2014, with the decision to keep it in storage in Indianapolis until appropriate facilities could be arranged in Sedona. The organ was moved to Sedona by the Organ Clearing House in November 2015, where it will share quarters with the famed Estey from Claremont College.

Chronicle

From Oberlin
to Sedona

  1. 1915

    An E. M. Skinner in Finney Chapel

    The organ whose pipework would later be folded into Opus 230-A is installed in Finney Memorial Chapel at Oberlin.

  2. 1955

    Aeolian-Skinner Opus 230-A

    The new instrument is installed in Finney Chapel, incorporating pipe sets from the historic Skinner that preceded it — the American Classic ideal at its height.

  3. 1955–1997

    Four decades of teaching

    Opus 230-A serves multiple generations of Conservatory organ students and visiting concert artists at one of the country’s great music schools.

  4. 1997

    A bequest changes everything

    Oberlin receives a significant bequest to purchase a large new instrument in a tonal style not yet part of the college’s organscape — a French-styled organ. With the only other hall large enough having just received a new Flentrop, Finney Chapel is chosen to receive it.

  5. 1999

    Out of Finney Chapel

    The instrument is sold to a congregation in the Episcopal Diocese of Virginia and moved from Oberlin to the Indianapolis organbuilders Goulding and Wood.

  6. June 2014

    Purchased for Sedona

    The Sedona Conservatory buys Opus 230-A, and decides to leave it in storage in Indianapolis until proper facilities can be arranged in Arizona.

  7. Nov 2015

    The move west

    The Organ Clearing House moves the organ to Sedona, where it takes up quarters alongside the famed Estey from Claremont.

  8. Now

    Waiting to speak again

    Its historic console is to be refurbished and playable on its own, as well as from the new five-manual console.
