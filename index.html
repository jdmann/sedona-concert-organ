Sedona, Arizona  ·  The Festival Concert Hall

The Sedona Concert Organ

Two landmark American organs, silent for a generation, are being raised again as a single instrument beneath the red rocks — 235 ranks, more than 12,700 pipes, and one console to command them all.

Enter the Nave Buy a Pipe

I.   The Instrument

A cathedral voice
in canyon country

The Sedona Conservatory Concert Hall Organ will total some 235 sets of pipes — ranks — comprising more than 12,700 individual pipes, making it by far the largest concert hall organ in the world. It is not an instrument assembled from a catalogue. It is an instrument gathered: two of the great American organs of the twentieth century, located, secured, and now being brought back to speech.

Where a cathedral organ answers stone, this one will answer sandstone. The Festival Concert Hall rises where the light comes off the rocks red at evening, and the instrument inside is scaled to that horizon — a full symphonic palette from the merest breath of a string to a full-organ chord that you feel in the floor before you hear it.

  • 235 Ranks of pipes
  • 12,700+ Individual pipes
  • 5 Manuals at the new console
  • 2 Landmark organs united

Largest concert hall pipe organ in the world  ·  a three-to-five-year organbuilding project, now under way

II.   Two Landmarks

The organs that
came before

Acquisition of the Conservatory's source instruments is complete. Both historic organs are being reestablished so that each may be played individually from its own magnificent original console — and both together from a new one.

1931

Estey Magnum
Opus 2981

Claremont  ·  Pomona College

Estey's Magnum Opus — the Brattleboro firm's grandest surviving statement, built in 1931 for Claremont and Pomona College. Vast in scale, orchestral in ambition, and voiced in an era when an American organ was expected to hold a hall by itself.

Reestablished to play from its original console.

The Estey’s story

1955

Aeolian-Skinner
Opus 230-A

Oberlin College Conservatory of Music

From the house of G. Donald Harrison, at the height of the American Classic ideal: clarity over weight, choruses that build like architecture, reeds that cut without shouting. For decades it taught organists at one of the country's great conservatories.

Reestablished to play from its original console.

The Skinner’s story

And one console to bind them

Five keyboards.
One instrument.

A new five-manual console is being designed to play both historic organs as one cohesive instrument — the Estey and the Aeolian-Skinner answering a single pair of hands across the width of the hall. Two centuries of American organbuilding, on one bench.

III.   Voices

The families
of the organ

A rank is a single row of pipes — one timbre, one pipe per note. Two hundred thirty-five of them, sorted into the four families every great organ is built upon, plus the percussions that make a hall glitter.

Principals

Diapason

The organ's own voice, belonging to no other instrument. Stacked in a chorus from 16′ to mixture, principals are what makes a room feel like a cathedral.

Flutes

Flauto

Open, stopped, harmonic, wooden. The colours that carry a melody over an accompaniment — and, at 32′, the pedal notes you feel rather than hear.

Strings

Viole · Céleste

Narrow-scaled and keen, drawn in pairs slightly out of tune with one another so the sound shimmers. The softest thing a great organ can do, and often the most moving.

Reeds

Trompette · Tuba

Brass of the organ. From the plaintive oboe to a Tuba on heavy wind that will ride over full organ and never be covered — the crown of the ensemble.

Percussions

Chimes · Harp · Celesta

Struck voices inherited from the great American builders — the bells and glitter that turn an organ into an orchestra.

The Room

Sedona

IV.   Chronicle

Ninety years,
one instrument

  1. 1931

    Estey builds its Magnum Opus

    Opus 2981 leaves Brattleboro, Vermont for Claremont and Pomona College.

  2. 1955

    Aeolian-Skinner Opus 230-A

    Installed at the Oberlin College Conservatory of Music, in the full flower of the American Classic school.

  3. Today

    Acquisition complete

    Both landmark organs are located and secured by the Sedona Conservatory — beginning the organbuilding project in earnest.

  4. 3–5 yrs

    The building of it

    Restoration, re-leathering, revoicing, and the design and construction of the new five-manual console.

  5. Then

    First sound in the hall

    235 ranks speak together for the first time, in the Festival Concert Hall.
